# CFPB and NCUA complaints about Navy Federal: drafts to paste in

**When to send:** only if Navy Federal hasn't fixed this by **Nov 13, 2026** (about 30 days after you reported it), or sooner if they refuse in writing. Send the CFPB complaint first. Navy Federal is a big credit union, so the CFPB is the main agency for this. The NCUA complaint is a backup and goes in if the CFPB doesn't get it resolved.

Before you submit:
- Fill every [BRACKET]. Delete this top section.
- Use **only the last 4 digits** of any account or loan. Never type a full account number, SSN, PIN or password.
- Leave out your FTC report number and claim numbers until the form asks for them, then type them into the form only. Don't add them to the board.
- Say "review" to Claude so the case reviewer can check the facts and dates first.

## 1. CFPB complaint (consumerfinance.gov/complaint)

**Pick on the form** (the menu wording changes sometimes, so choose the closest match):
- **Company:** Navy Federal Credit Union.
- **Product:** *Payday loan, title loan, personal loan, or advance loan* → personal or installment loan. If the 3115 loan was a car loan, pick *Vehicle loan or lease* instead.
- **Issue:** the choice closest to **fraud, identity theft, or a loan I didn't take out**.
- **The Oct 9–10 charges go in a separate, second complaint:** use *Checking or savings account* if they were on your debit card, or *Credit card* if they were on a Navy Federal credit card. Then choose the issue about **unauthorized transactions**. Paste the card paragraph below into that one.

## 2. NCUA complaint (mycreditunion.gov → Consumer Assistance Center → file a complaint)

The NCUA usually wants you to complain to the credit union first. You've done that, so say so. For a credit union as large as Navy Federal, the NCUA may send part of your complaint over to the CFPB. That's normal, so file at the CFPB first and give the NCUA your CFPB complaint number.

**Credit union:** Navy Federal Credit Union, Vienna, VA

**Complaint (paste):**

> I am filing a complaint against Navy Federal Credit Union about how it is handling identity theft on my membership.
>
> A $1,700 loan ending 3115 was opened in my name on 08/21/2025 without my knowledge or permission. I reported it to Navy Federal as fraud on [DATE], along with unauthorized transactions on my [card type] ending [LAST 4] from October 9–10, 2026. I have filed an FTC Identity Theft Report ([DATE]), sent identity theft blocks to all three credit bureaus ([DATE]), frozen my credit, and sent Navy Federal its claim form and supporting documents ([DATE]).
>
> More than 30 days later, Navy Federal has [not resolved the claim / denied it without explanation / continued to report the loan or seek payment]. I also filed CFPB complaint number [CFPB #] on [DATE].
>
> I am asking the NCUA to ensure Navy Federal: closes the fraudulent loan and confirms I owe nothing; removes it from my credit reports; stops collection; gives me the application and payment records I am entitled to as an identity theft victim; and refunds the unauthorized transactions.

**What I have already done to resolve it with the credit union (paste):**

> [DATE]: Reported the loan and card charges by [phone / branch / secure message]. Claim reference given: yes / no (type it on the form only).
> [DATE]: Sent Navy Federal claim form and packet.
> [DATE]: [Any follow-up call or answer.]

**Attach:** the same documents as the CFPB complaint, plus the CFPB confirmation page.

## 3. After you send

1. Save the confirmation page or number for each complaint. Keep them off the board and in your private vault.
2. The CFPB sends your complaint to Navy Federal, and they usually answer within **15 days** (60 at most). You'll see their answer in your CFPB account and can say whether you're satisfied.
3. Keep paying close attention to your credit reports at annualcreditreport.com. If the loan is still there after the bureaus' 30-day dispute window, tell Claude and we'll do the next step.
4. A Better Business Bureau complaint is optional and only after these.
